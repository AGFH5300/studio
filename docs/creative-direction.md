# Veya Labs — creative direction

Session 1 · 6 September 2026 · Proposed master direction, ready for a visual feasibility phase

## Decision

Build **The Veya Fold**: a digital product that opens to reveal, configure and demonstrate the business system inside it. A visitor moves between a beautifully finished website and its functioning interior. Their choices reshape that same product. The estimator is the act of making it their own.

Working public proposition: **Beautiful websites. Built to do business.** Supporting sentence: “Websites, ecommerce and intelligent integrations for UAE businesses.” Keep “Projects from AED 999” in immediately visible HTML. Primary action: **Build your project**. Secondary: **See our work**. “Open the Fold” is an interaction label, never the only label for the estimator.

The world is a close, crafted product stage, with material depth and architectural composition. It is not a laboratory building. The signature object is an unusually wide, hinged digital folio: a finished interface held in a slender porcelain-and-metal frame, connected through one offset folding spine to additional page surfaces and small working service mechanisms. All parts have supports, hinges or tracks. Avoid floating glass cards and a grid of UI panels disguised as 3D.

## Three fundamentally different concepts

### 1. The Living Atelier

- **Core idea / fit:** enter a continuous working studio whose stations demonstrate design, commerce and automation. Veya is a team making things, not a fictional science organization.
- **User action:** select a station from a clear map or direct navigation; inspect a project; operate a working sample; bring selected capabilities to a central project bench.
- **Hero:** a beautifully lit cutaway studio with one active fabrication table. A finished page is assembled as the visitor arrives. Business copy remains outside the environment.
- **Estimator:** a project travels between stations, gaining pages, editorial tools and systems. A conventional form stays synchronized.
- **Work:** project alcoves with explorable exhibits; each has a directly linked HTML case study.
- **Visual language / ceiling:** warm architectural realism, strong shadow boundaries, joined rooms, sculpted furniture, spatial sound optional. Extremely high ceiling with credible inhabitance.
- **Technical approach:** authored Blender room kit, streamed GLBs, constrained cameras, R3F and DOM overlays; no walking controller needed.
- **Business effectiveness:** understandable craft metaphor but visitors may remember the room more than the services. Requires direct shortcuts everywhere.
- **Mobile:** selected station fills a compact view above its controls, with explicit next/previous station buttons.
- **Risks:** the building becomes the project; several beautiful rooms multiply asset and QA costs; it resembles the suggested digital studio too literally. Station travel can turn scope selection into waiting.

### 2. The Veya Fold — selected

- **Core idea / fit:** the website itself is the exhibit. Open its surface, inspect its systems, change its scope, run a customer journey and close it into a finished experience.
- **User action:** unfold, change, try, compare and take away a project brief. Every major interaction corresponds to something Veya sells or delivers.
- **Hero:** a single captivating digital folio on a dark, shallow stage. Its screen contains a tasteful sample business website. “See what makes it work” opens the folio into a spatial composition of connected surfaces and mechanisms.
- **Estimator:** the folio becomes the visitor’s project; pages fan along its spine; CMS creates one editable source whose change appears in two views; commerce adds an order path; AI can interpret an enquiry; automation routes the confirmed result. The folio closes into a preview and a real scope summary.
- **Work:** each project uses the same front/inside/reality grammar: finished experience, system explanation, actual evidence. A preview expands into a normal case study without navigating a game map.
- **Visual language / ceiling:** restrained product cinematography with architectural depth, broad warm key light, graphite stage, ivory surfaces, cobalt edge detail, polished-but-not-mirrored metal. A recognisable folding silhouette, not a generic laptop mockup.
- **Technical approach:** a small authored hinge/frame kit plus procedural page geometry; one renderer, shared scene manifest, deterministic state; DOM handles readable content and all forms. Layer separation and camera approach create scale without a city.
- **Business effectiveness:** it literally demonstrates website + systems; visitors retain a useful scoped brief. It can explain why a simple site and a complex product have different starting investments.
- **Mobile:** a frontal folio with one plane unfolded at a time; tap to open, edit or run a sample; a compact illustration accompanies full-size HTML controls.
- **Risks:** can degrade into an exploded stack of feature cards; miniature text may be illegible; “build” may imply an instant website generator. Require functional demonstrations, joined geometry, readable close-ups and explicit scope-preview copy.

### 3. The Business Rehearsal

- **Core idea / fit:** a business operating inside an interactive editorial stage. Visitors rehearse a customer’s experience and change the systems that support it.
- **User action:** choose a business scenario; become its customer for a moment; book, enquire or place a sample order; turn capabilities on and watch the outcome change.
- **Hero:** split the view between a customer-facing site and a carefully choreographed operations stage. A request creates visible, readable events rather than glowing abstract particles.
- **Estimator:** selecting outcomes assembles a rehearsal: attract an enquiry, qualify it, reserve a slot, follow up. Scope and package suitability emerge from the selected functions.
- **Work:** replay a real project’s key workflow with annotated design and implementation choices; then read the evidence.
- **Visual language / ceiling:** cinematic 2D/2.5D, tactile typography, precise diagrams, editorial crops and live interfaces; less architectural spectacle but potentially exceptional interaction intelligence.
- **Technical approach:** semantic React UI, SVG/canvas transitions, small optional 3D product assets, deterministic event replay; API-backed AI in a bounded demo.
- **Business effectiveness:** strongest direct explanation of operational value, especially for nontechnical buyers.
- **Mobile:** native vertical sequence of customer action, result and next action. Excellent translation.
- **Risks:** can feel like a SaaS product tour or a diagramming tool; harder to achieve the immediate environmental reaction the brief prioritizes; branches may overwhelm visitors before they know the offering.

The Fold wins because spectacle, scope selection and evidence belong to the same object. Its advantage is not simply fewer assets: its hero interaction is also its explanation of the business. The Rehearsal’s causal demonstration is adopted inside the Fold; its split-screen world and navigation are not. The Atelier’s multi-room environment is rejected.

Scores remain a hypothesis. If the next phase cannot make a Fold silhouette distinctive and the unfold sequence understandable, revise the visual form before extending it across the site. Do not compensate with particles, more rooms or a louder palette.

## Visual system to execute

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Canvas / stage | Ink graphite #171B20, a shallow real surface, soft rear gradient caused by lighting; no starfield |
| Main surfaces | Warm porcelain #E9E6DE with subtle roughness and visible bevels |
| Text | Ink #172027 on pale areas, off-white #F4F3EE on dark areas |
| Accent | Cobalt #365CF5 on active edges and focus; amber #D89A52 only for one travelling request token; verify actual contrast before use |
| Typography | Candidate self-hosted Manrope for UI and headings; IBM Plex Mono only for brief IDs or technical annotations. Verify font licenses and weights at implementation |
| Scale | Body 16–18px, desktop hero 44–64px, mobile hero 32–40px. Never a viewport-sized headline. Labels at least 14px |
| Layout | 8px spacing rhythm; desktop 12-column grid, 48–80px outer margins at large widths; mobile 20px; prose max 65–72 characters |
| Controls | Solid surfaces, thin purposeful borders, 8–12px corners, explicit pressed/selected states; no frosted stack |
| Geometry | One asymmetric spine, radiused rectangular frames, shallow chamfered inserts, visible joints; maximum three fully readable page faces at once |
| Materials | Porcelain roughness ~0.45–0.65; metal ~0.25–0.4. Starting artist values, not fixed physical measurements. No chrome floor or bloom wash |
| Light | Broad warm key from upper left, soft cool fill, controlled edge reflection. Contact shadows establish attachment; one visual focal point |
| Camera | Near-orthographic 3/4 opening view, initially 35–45 degrees elevation. Limited ±12 degree exploratory orbit. Close-up named views; no free-fly, roll or mandatory pointer lock |
| Motion | Controls 120–180ms; local changes 220–400ms; signature unfold 900–1400ms, interruptible. One master timeline owns camera and joints |
| Icons | Small coherent SVG set using readable business meanings; no generated raster icons for essential UI |
| Screens | Proper layouts with authored images and real miniature interactions; no fake code streams, meaningless graphs or stock dashboard wallpaper |

Responsive screen previews are honest examples, not promises of the visitor’s final design. Offer two clearly different design treatments using the same content and capability set. Visual theme never silently changes a paid design tier.

## Four brand moments

1. **The opening:** one finished website opens along its offset spine into pages and connected capabilities; its visual identity survives the transformation.
2. **One edit, everywhere:** changing a CMS headline updates a page and a phone preview together, showing content reuse.
3. **Follow an enquiry:** a readable sample enquiry is classified, passed to a CRM record and given a draft follow-up; the visitor can inspect each result. Clearly labelled demo data.
4. **Fold it into your brief:** the system closes into a finished front face while a clean HTML scope summary appears; share/copy exports the same choices.

No launch soundscape. Optional subtle sound can be reconsidered only after silent interaction is excellent; never autoplay. Optional easter egg: an unobtrusive maker’s stamp opens a concise “How this site works” panel, after all business paths exist. No collecting badges or hidden-price rewards.

## Success tests and acceptance

- **Showcase:** a 15-second capture containing unfold → change → request journey makes sense without narration. A standalone still has one clear focus and no exposed debug UI.
- **Business:** in a five-second static view, a first-time visitor can identify websites/integrations, UAE focus, AED 999 entry and how to start. Prices accessible in one navigation action.
- **Identity:** with the logo removed, the offset spine, folding behavior and material vocabulary still distinguish the site. Check against a generic exploded-device mockup; if they look equivalent, redesign the object.

These tests are proposed validation gates, not claims that an unbuilt design has already passed.
